# Writ prototype

Read `../writ/rules.md` first.

Some questions are answered faster by building than by talking. Build enough to choose.

1. **Name the fork.** Say in one sentence what is undecided, then the two or three ways it could go that would change what users experience. Three colours of one idea is not a fork. State them and carry on; do not wait for approval.

2. **Build the drafts.** One helper per draft, each starting fresh so the drafts do not converge. Tell each the goal, its direction, and to keep the draft small and runnable. Use the project's real stack so the winner can be kept. Two or three drafts; more only if the person asks.

3. **Show them.** Put the drafts where the person can try them side by side: running, opened, or as screenshots when nothing else works. For each, say what it is like to use, what it is good at, and what it gives up. Then say which you would pick and why.

4. **The person picks.** This is taste, so it is theirs. They may pick one, blend two, or reject all of them. If they reject all, ask what was missing and go round once more.

5. **Record the choice** in `.writ/prototypes/<date>-<name>/choice.md`: the fork, each draft in a line, which won, and why the others lost. Delete the losing drafts from the code. The record keeps the lesson.

6. **Then:** if the winner is small and complete, finish it the way `../writ/SKILL.md` describes for a small change. Otherwise hand the choice to `writ-spec` as its starting point.

If the fork is a fact and not a preference (which is faster, does it fit, does the service allow it), build the smallest thing that measures it, report the result, and skip the choosing.
